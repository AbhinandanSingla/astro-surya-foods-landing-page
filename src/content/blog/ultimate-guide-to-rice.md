Rice is one of the most important staple foods in the world, feeding billions of people every day and shaping cuisines across the globe. From a fragrant plate of biryani to a comforting bowl of risotto, this naturally gluten-free grain lends itself to an endless number of recipes.

However, many simply think of rice as just "white" or "brown." In reality, there is a massive variety of grains, each with its own unique flavor, texture, cooking behavior, and nutritional value. Understanding these characteristics is essential for home cooks, restaurant owners, and food businesses alike to ensure the right grain is used for the right dish.

This guide examines the factors that differentiate types of rice — from nutty Basmati to fragrant Jasmine — so you can choose the best rice for your next recipe.

## Is Rice a Grain?

Yes! Rice is one of the **eight true cereal grains**, along with wheat, oats, corn (maize), barley, rye, millet, and sorghum. Each grain of rice consists of four distinct parts:

- **Hull (Husk):** The tough outer shell enclosing the grain. This is removed in all commercially available rice types.
- **Bran:** The nutritious layer under the hull. It can be tan, reddish, or black depending on the variety. It is left intact in brown and whole-grain rice but removed for white rice.
- **Endosperm (White Rice):** The starchy center left behind once the bran and germ are milled away. This is the part most commonly consumed.
- **Germ:** The nutrient-dense rice kernel found under the hull, packed with B vitamins, minerals, and proteins. It helps give rice its color and added nutritional benefits.

## How to Cook Rice: 5 Foolproof Methods

### 1. Stovetop Method
- **Equipment:** Pot with lid
- **Ingredients:**
  - 1 part rice (e.g. 1 cup)
  - 1-2 parts water/liquid (depending on type)
  - Pinch of salt (optional)
- **Instructions:**
  1. Rinse the rice under cold water until the water runs clear.
  2. Combine the rice, water, and salt in the pot.
  3. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat.
  4. Once boiling, reduce the heat to low, cover, and simmer for 18-20 minutes.
  5. Remove from heat and let it sit, covered, for 5 minutes.
  6. Fluff with a fork and serve.

### 2. Rice Cooker Method
- **Equipment:** Rice cooker (or multi-cooker)
- **Ingredients:**
  - 1 part rice (e.g. 1 cup)
  - 1.5 parts water/liquid (or per rice cooker instructions)
- **Instructions:**
  1. Rinse the rice under cold water until the water runs clear.
  2. Add the rice and water to the rice cooker.
  3. Close the lid and start the cooker.
  4. The cooker will automatically switch to 'keep warm' once the rice is done, which usually takes about 20-25 minutes.
  5. Let the rice sit for an additional 5-10 minutes before fluffing with a fork and serving.

### 3. Instant Pot / Pressure Cooker Method
- **Equipment:** Instant pot (or pressure cooker)
- **Ingredients:**
  - 1 part rice (e.g. 1 cup)
  - 1 part water/liquid (e.g. 1 cup)
  - Pinch of salt (optional)
- **Instructions:**
  1. Rinse the rice under cold water until the water runs clear.
  2. Add the rice, water, and salt to the Instant Pot.
  3. Seal the lid and set the valve to the sealing position.
  4. Cook on high pressure for 3 minutes.
  5. Allow a natural pressure release for 10 minutes, then manually release any remaining pressure.
  6. Fluff with a fork and serve.

### 4. Microwave Method
- **Equipment:** Microwave-safe bowl with lid
- **Ingredients:**
  - 1 part rice (e.g. 1 cup)
  - 1-2 parts water/liquid (depending on type)
  - Pinch of salt (optional)
- **Instructions:**
  1. Rinse the rice under cold water until the water runs clear.
  2. Combine the rice, water, and salt in the microwave-safe bowl.
  3. Cover and microwave on high for 10 minutes.
  4. Stir, cover again, and microwave on medium power for another 10-15 minutes.
  5. Let it sit, covered, for 5 minutes before fluffing with a fork and serving.

### 5. Baked Rice Method
- **Equipment:** Oven-safe dish with lid or foil
- **Ingredients:**
  - 1 part rice (e.g. 1 cup)
  - 1.5 parts boiling water (e.g. 1.5 cups)
  - Pinch of salt (optional)
- **Instructions:**
  1. Preheat the oven to 190°C (375°F).
  2. Rinse the rice under cold water until the water runs clear.
  3. Add the rice to the oven-safe dish.
  4. Pour the boiling water and salt over the rice.
  5. Cover tightly with a lid or foil.
  6. Bake for 25-30 minutes.
  7. Let it sit, covered, for 5 minutes before fluffing with a fork and serving.

## Rice by Color: Nutrition at a Glance

The color of rice is a great indicator of its nutritional profile and culinary application.

- **White Rice:** Easy to digest, high in simple carbohydrates, and offers a neutral base that absorbs sauces and flavors beautifully. The most commonly consumed type worldwide.
- **Brown Rice:** A whole grain with bran and germ intact. Higher in fiber, vitamins, and minerals than white rice. Chewier texture and nuttier flavor.
- **Red Rice:** An heirloom variety loaded with dietary fiber and anthocyanins, known to help lower cholesterol and blood pressure.
- **Black (Forbidden) Rice:** The most nutrient-dense variety, rich in anthocyanins, iron, and protein. Sweet and aromatic with a distinctive dark color.
- **Yellow Rice:** Long-grain white rice flavored and colored with saffron or turmeric. Popular in South American, South African, Indian, and Southeast Asian cuisines.

## Rice FAQs

**Do you have to wash rice before cooking?**

Yes — rinsing your rice prior to cooking eliminates any residual dust, helps give it a **consistent texture**, and gets rid of excess surface starch that can make rice gummy. Generally, it is a good idea to rinse most types of rice, but always check the packaging first. Some rice comes enriched with water-soluble vitamins and minerals, which will dissolve if rinsed.

**What is polished rice?**

Polished rice (another name for white rice or milled rice) is rice that has been milled to **remove the husk, bran, and germ** and is polished to have a shiny white color. It cooks much faster than unpolished (brown) rice but has lower nutritional value.

**What is converted rice?**

Converted rice, also known as parboiled rice, is a type of rice that has been **partially boiled and dried**, which allows the rice to retain more nutrients compared to ordinary white rice.

**What are the two subtypes of Asian rice?**

There are two subtypes: **Indica rice** — more commonly eaten, featuring a long, slim grain and aromatic profile — and **Japonica rice** — featuring a short to medium grain, little aroma, and a distinctly sticky texture when cooked.

**What is the difference between Sella and Steam rice?**

**Sella (parboiled) rice** is soaked, partially boiled, and dried before milling, giving it a golden color and firmer texture. **Steam rice** is lightly steamed before milling, resulting in a softer texture closer to raw rice. Both methods improve shelf life and grain strength during milling.
